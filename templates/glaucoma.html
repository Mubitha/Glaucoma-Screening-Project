Understanding Childhood Glaucoma

Childhood glaucoma is a rare but serious eye condition that affects infants and young children. Early detection is critical to prevent permanent vision loss.

OVERVIEW

What is Childhood Glaucoma?

Childhood glaucoma occurs when the fluid pressure inside a child’s eye increases due to improper drainage. This pressure damages the optic nerve, which is essential for vision. Unlike adult glaucoma, childhood glaucoma progresses rapidly and requires early screening.

CAUSES

Main Causes

  • Abnormal development of eye drainage channels
  • Genetic inheritance from parents
  • Congenital eye defects
  • Eye trauma or infections
SYMPTOMS

Warning Signs

  • Enlarged or cloudy cornea
  • Excessive tearing
  • Extreme sensitivity to light
  • Red or swollen eyes
RISK FACTORS

Who is at Risk?

Children with a family history of glaucoma, premature birth, or genetic disorders are at higher risk. Regular eye screening is recommended for high-risk children.

How Glaucoma Progresses

1

Fluid drainage problem begins inside the eye

2

Eye pressure gradually increases

3

Optic nerve gets damaged

4

Permanent vision loss if untreated
